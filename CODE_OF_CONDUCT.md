# Code of conduct

I want honestweek to be a project where anyone can ask a question, report a bug or send a change without being made to feel small. This page says what I expect from everyone who takes part, me included, and what happens when that goes wrong.

It's based on the [Contributor Covenant, version 2.1](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/). This page is the short version in my own words. Where it leaves something out, the Covenant's full text is what I go by.

## What I expect

- Be kind and patient, especially with people who are new to the project or to open source.
- Assume good intent, and ask a question before you assume an answer.
- Disagree with the work, not the person. Say what's wrong with a change and how it could be better.
- Take a "no" on a change gracefully. When I say no, I'll say why.
- When you get something wrong, own it, apologize to whoever it affected, and learn from it.
- Respect other people's privacy. This project exists to keep session logs private, and that goes for the people around it too.

## What isn't okay

- Sexual language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention of any kind.
- Insults, put-downs, trolling, and personal or political attacks.
- Harassment, in public or in private.
- Posting someone else's private information without their explicit permission: an address, an email, a private message, or anything from their session logs.
- Anything else a reasonable person would see as out of place in a professional setting.

## Where it applies

Everywhere the project lives: issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, commit messages, and anywhere you speak for the project in public.

## Reporting a problem

If someone's behavior here worries you, please tell me privately. Don't put the details in a public issue.

- Reach me through the contact options on [my GitHub profile](https://github.com/BryceEWatson).
- If you'd rather not, open an issue titled "Conduct report" with no details in it, and I'll get in touch to take it private.

If the problem is with me, or it's serious enough that GitHub should know, open the `...` menu on the comment, issue or pull request and choose **Report content**. That goes to GitHub's own team, not to me.

A security problem, like a way honestweek could leak data off your machine, isn't a conduct report. Please send it through the private reporting described in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).

I'll read every report, keep it private, and reply as soon as I can. I won't share who reported something without their permission.

## What happens next

I follow the Covenant's enforcement guidelines. Depending on what happened, that's one of these:

1. **A correction.** A private note saying what was wrong and why. Sometimes a public apology is the right fix.
2. **A warning.** A clear warning, and a break from talking with the people involved for a while. Repeating the behavior can lead to a ban.
3. **A temporary ban.** No interaction with the project, in public or in private, for a set time.
4. **A permanent ban.** For a pattern of harmful behavior, or one serious incident such as harassment.

I may also edit, hide or remove comments, commits, code, issues and other contributions that break this code.
